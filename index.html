One card at a time is a queue you can audit

An unlimited-requests plan still moves one card at a time, and that single number is the real unit of account. The board runs Requests, Active, Approved with one card in Active, and the 72-hour turnaround sets the pace: 21 working days at three days a card is about seven cards a month. At $5,995 a month that is about $856 per delivered card, a figure you can recompute any morning from your own board.

The batch shop hides the same work in a queue. Suppose all seven cards ship together on the last working day of the month. Ages at ship run 21, 18, 15, 12, 9, 6, and 3 working days, an average of 12, so the month holds 84 card-days of waiting against 21 when each card ships three days after it arrives. The 63 extra card-days never appear on an invoice. They show up as "still waiting on the final files."

The one-card rule buys visibility. A card that balloons sits in Active where you can see it every day and cut scope before it doubles, and revisions run per card until it clears. Onboarding onto the board runs about 24 hours, so the count starts this week; on the yearly plan the same seven-card pace is $4,995 a month, about $714 per card, and the queue math is unchanged.
